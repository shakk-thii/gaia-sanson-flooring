Why does sunlight fade artificial grass?

Artificial grass blades are polymer fibres coloured with pigment. Ultraviolet light breaks both down over time, which shows first as a paler, bleached green and later as a drier, more brittle blade. UV stabilisers built into the yarn slow that process, which is why two lawns of the same green can look very different after a few years in the same sun.

Heat adds to the strain, and so does sunlight reflected from windows and glass railings, which can concentrate on one patch of lawn for part of the day.

What can a UV test tell you, and what can it not?

A weathering test is a laboratory benchmark. It shows how a yarn stands up to accelerated sun and weather compared with others, but it cannot promise a set number of years on your terrace, because no laboratory reproduces your orientation, glare and use. What on site makes artificial grass fade faster?

Most of what you can control is here rather than in the product choice.

  • Hours of direct sun. A south- or west-facing terrace with no shade takes far more UV than a shaded courtyard. Shade on the hottest side helps, whether from a pergola, a shade sail or planting.
  • Reflected sunlight. Windows, glass balustrades and glazed façades can bounce concentrated light onto the grass. Watch the site across a sunny day before deciding where the lawn goes.
  • Flattened pile. Areas that are walked on constantly, or sit under furniture, lie flat and catch the light differently. People often mistake this for fading; brushing the pile back up usually restores the look.
  • Dust. A dusty lawn looks dull and pale long before the colour has changed. A regular rinse, where drainage allows, brings the true green back.

Is surface heat the same problem as fading?

No, though the same sun drives both. Fading is a slow change in the yarn; heat is how the lawn feels underfoot on a summer afternoon. Like any synthetic surface in direct sun, artificial grass warms up more than a natural lawn, which cools itself by releasing moisture. Shade over seating and play areas helps with both, and a quick rinse of cool water brings the surface temperature down before use.
